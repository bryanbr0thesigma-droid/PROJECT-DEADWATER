# PROJECT: DEADWATER: lore (draft)

Status: brainstorm. Nothing here is final, and none of it is in the game yet unless it says so.

**In one line:** F.A.L.C.O.N. shows a friendly, safety-first face. Underneath, Project DEADWATER
has spent twenty years turning people into things that live in the water, and the Wader is one of
them.

Tone: a cheerful organisation hiding human experiments, found out through its own old tapes,
memos and leftover marketing. We take that *shape* only. The names, events, places and creatures
are ours, and none of them should echo another game's.

---

## F.A.L.C.O.N.'s two faces

| What recruits are told | What's really going on |
|---|---|
| The Federal Agency for Liminal Classification & Occult Nature studies strange places. | A cover story. It "classifies" what it made. |
| The Wader is "Liminal Entity L-7". | Internally, L stands for **Lot**: Lot 7 of Project DEADWATER. The lobby's field guide ("Vol. 1: L-1 to L-9") lists nine people. |
| The orientation tapes keep you safe. | They keep you working and calm. They're read by a machine voice, so no one on staff has to say the words. |
| Mineral water powers the generators. | The generators are pumps, and the canteens are the harvest. Every round, you work for them. |
| "Water must not leave the poolrooms." | That rule is for you. F.A.L.C.O.N. ships it out by the tanker. |
| Pension, dental, "grief counselling". | Losses are budgeted. Each recruit has a line item. |
| "Taken personnel sometimes come back. Do not ask them about the water." | They come back part-way to becoming a Lot. |
| "If the count is higher, do not tell them. Inform your supervisor." | Supervisors collect the ones who come back, for the project. |

## Deadwater

"Dead water" is real: at sea, a heavy layer of water hiding under a lighter one grabs ships and
holds them in place. In the poolrooms there's a warm, heavy layer under the pool water. It glows
faintly where it rises to the surface: the teal mineral patches the canteens are filled from.

If you soak in it long enough, it replaces the water in your body (about 60% of you). You start to
hear through water and stop needing your eyes, and dry air starts to hurt. That's why the Wader
hunts by sound and never leaves the water. It's also why tape 07-W ends: *"The human body is
about sixty percent water. It can hear yours."*

## History

- **1920s to 1960s: Pinewater Springs Resort & Mineral Baths.** "Take the Pinewater cure." The
  station's lobby is the old hotel reception, which is why it looks like a hotel.
  - Chlora-Cola starts as the resort's tonic soda, made with spring water. Real early sodas were
    sold as medicine; 7 Up first contained lithium.
  - Guests who stay too long change. They turn pale, they're always cold on dry land, and they
    sleep in the baths.
- **1971: F.A.L.C.O.N. takes over.** It closes the resort "for renovation" and keeps the reception
  as Pinewater Field Station.
  - Project DEADWATER begins. Can deadwater make people who live and hunt underwater?
  - The buyers would want divers who don't need air, and sentries who can hear a footstep a mile
    off.
- **1970s and 80s: the Lots.** The first subjects are staff volunteers, then "wellness retreat"
  guests, then recruits from the Training Division. Nine reach the end: Lots L-1 to L-9.
  - **L-7, the Wader, was the resort's lifeguard.** It still patrols the shallow pools where it
    taught swimming lessons.
- **1988: the Turnover.** (In pool terms, the turnover is the time it takes for every drop to pass
  through the filters once.)
  - A pump test runs backwards. Deadwater floods the lower baths, the Lots get loose and staff
    are lost.
  - Afterwards the halls don't end: the deadwater keeps building rooms outwards. The "endless
    poolrooms" are the project's spill, not a natural place.
  - F.A.L.C.O.N. seals the lower doors and calls it a liminal anomaly.
- **Now (the 90s): Training Division teams.** Recruits are sent in to "restore power".
  - What they really do is harvest deadwater, which still sells: in Chlora-Cola, and to buyers.
  - F.A.L.C.O.N. also watches which recruits soak it up best. Those get "promoted".

## The Wader's clicks are a lifeguard's whistle

Lifeguards really do signal with whistle blasts: one short blast to get a swimmer's attention, two
to call another guard, and three or one long blast for an emergency. The Wader still does it,
with clicks:

- **patrol:** single clicks (watching the swimmers);
- **investigate:** double clicks (calling for backup that never comes);
- **charge:** three, or one long rattle (emergency).

The clicks come from a real person's voice (the takes recorded for the game), which fits:
something human trying to signal. Players can learn the code and read the creature by ear. A late
tape or a lesson board in the Shallow Halls can teach it outright.

## The whistleblower

**Dr. M. Halloran** (placeholder name) is a project hydrologist who stayed after the Turnover.
Halloran records confessions on blank VHS tapes and hides them in the levels. Players bring them
back to the lobby VCR (which already exists). Halloran knows every Lot's real name, and gives
the players one human voice against F.A.L.C.O.N.

To decide later: is Halloran still alive, or the extra person in the count?

## How it gets into the game

- **The sign-in book remembers the dead.** When a player dies in a round, their name appears in
  the lobby's register with no time out ("Personnel who do not sign out are considered still
  inside"). Cheap, personal, and a good first piece to build.
- **Hidden tapes:** Halloran's confessions, collected in the levels and played in the lobby.
- **New lobby tapes:**
  - Tape 09, a cheerful internal promo selling DEADWATER to buyers;
  - Tape 00, damaged footage from 1974.
- **Props:** clipboards, memos, Lot files and resort brochures (the named-prop items already
  exist), plus the Chlora-Cola can's small print: "Contains mineral water (Pinewater source)."
- **Be the monster (idea):** you play a Lot, which now has a reason in the story.
