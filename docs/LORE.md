# PROJECT: DEADWATER: lore (draft)

Status: brainstorm. Nothing here is final, and none of it is in the game yet unless it says so.

**In one line:** F.A.L.C.O.N. didn't make the poolrooms. It found them, and it has been cashing in
ever since. The people who find out what it's really doing don't come back. What's left of them
is still down there, in places you can see but will never reach.

Tone: a cheerful organisation hiding something rotten. You find out through its own tapes, memos and
marketing, and its voice never cracks however bad the truth gets. We take that *shape* only. The
names, events, places and creatures are ours, and none of them should echo another game's.

---

## What happened

- **1920s to 1960s: Pinewater Springs Resort & Mineral Baths.** "Take the Pinewater cure." The
  station's lobby is the old hotel reception, which is why it looks like a hotel. Guests sometimes
  vanish from the deep bath late at night, and the resort hushes it up.
- **1971: F.A.L.C.O.N. finds out why.** Behind the deep bath's drain grate, the pools just keep
  going. F.A.L.C.O.N. buys the resort, closes it "for renovation", and builds Pinewater Field
  Station over the way in.
- **The poolrooms were already there, and so was the Wader.** Nobody made them. The Wader is
  Liminal Entity L-7, one of the things F.A.L.C.O.N. has catalogued down there (the lobby's field
  guide lists L-1 to L-9).
- **Project DEADWATER.** F.A.L.C.O.N.'s name for making the poolrooms pay.

## What they're exploiting

**Deadwater.** "Dead water" is real: at sea, a heavy layer of water hiding under a lighter one grabs
ships and holds them in place. In the poolrooms there's a warm, heavy layer under the pool water,
glowing faintly where it rises: the teal mineral patches the canteens are filled from. It's
worth a fortune, and it can only be fetched by hand, because pumps upset the water and bring the
Wader.

What they sell it as (pick one or more):

| Use | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Power** | It runs the generators in the game. One canteen runs a building for a week. |
| **A product** | It's in Chlora-Cola ("It's the wet thing!"), the old resort tonic, still sold nationwide. |
| **Listening** (my favourite) | Deadwater carries sound perfectly. A glass of it on a desk works as a microphone that can be heard from the poolrooms. F.A.L.C.O.N. sells it to buyers who like to listen. It also explains the end of tape 07-W: *"The human body is about sixty percent water. It can hear yours."* |

**The recruits.** The Training Division is cheap labour. Teams carry deadwater out by the canteen,
and losses are budgeted (pension, dental, "grief counselling").

**The Wader.** F.A.L.C.O.N. doesn't fight it; it uses it. Anyone who becomes a problem can be
sent in alone, and the Wader does the rest. No body, no questions.

## The Reassigned

Whoever finds out is "**reassigned to Deep Survey**". In practice they're sent into a sealed
sector alone, without the orientation, where the Wader is. The paperwork stays polite: "Thank you
for volunteering for Deep Survey! Your locker will be cleared for the next recruit."

**What's left of them is in areas players can never access.** Every level should have at least one:

- a hand through a drain grate, under water you can't reach;
- a torn suit (the same kind the players wear) on a ledge across a gap no one could jump;
- an ID badge caught on the far side of a locked chain-link fence;
- a clipboard floating far out in an unlit pool, still readable through the lidar;
- something behind thick observation glass in a sealed staff corridor;
- a canteen strap tied to a ladder that goes down into pitch black, with the ladder cut off below.

They're never explained on the spot. Later, a personnel file with the same name and a REASSIGNED
stamp tells you who that was. The kill effects already leave torn pieces in the game, so these
vignettes can reuse them.

## The iceberg: the deeper you dig, the more you find

| Depth | What you find | F.A.L.C.O.N.'s voice |
|---|---|---|
| **0. The brochure** (the lobby) | The warm lobby, recruitment posters, a pension plan, Chlora-Cola, caring orientation tapes. | "We keep you safe." |
| **1. The fine print** (the Shallow Halls, in plain sight) | Sign-in entries with no time out. "Wet footprints? Report them." Grief counselling "for your family". The rules start to protect F.A.L.C.O.N., not you. | Friendly, a little off. |
| **2. The memos** (the Deep End, staff rooms) | People become numbers: "Team 14: 6 canteens. Losses: 2. Acceptable." The pipes go *up*. Chlora-Cola's small print: "Contains mineral water (Pinewater source)." | "Remember to thank your team for their contribution! 🙂" |
| **3. The business** (locked rooms, cabinets) | Project DEADWATER. Buyers' contracts. A sales tape. Losses that weren't accidents: teams sent where the Wader was. | A smiley sticker on an invoice. |
| **4. The Reassigned** (hidden, hard to reach) | Personnel files stamped REASSIGNED, matching the remains you've glimpsed. The whistleblower's tapes. | "Thank you for volunteering for Deep Survey!" |
| **5. The bottom** (only for players who dig hardest) | A reassignment list with **your team on it**: "Training Division team. Flagged: unauthorised access. Reassign after next yield." Now you know, and so do they. | "Congratulations on your assignment." |

The rules that make this work:

- **Physically deeper means a deeper secret.** Each level goes further down, under the resort.
- **The front never cracks.** The darker the find, the cheerier the packaging.
- **Digging is optional.** The main path shows layers 0 to 2; layers 3 to 5 are for players who
  search (hidden tapes, vents, codes, locked cabinets).
- **Each find changes what you've already seen.** Rewatch tape 07-W after layer 5, and "Thank you
  for your service" means something else.

## The whistleblower

**Dr. M. Halloran** (placeholder name) is a hydrologist on Project DEADWATER who recorded the truth
on blank VHS tapes and hid them in the levels. Players bring them back to the lobby VCR (which
already exists). Halloran was reassigned. Players who collect every tape learn where: one of the
unreachable vignettes is Halloran's.

## The Wader's clicks

The tapes say "Listen for its clicking". F.A.L.C.O.N.'s field notes break the clicks into a code
the players can learn:

- **patrol:** single clicks;
- **investigate:** double clicks;
- **charge:** three, or one long rattle.

## Each level is part of the old resort, further down each time

| Level | It used to be | Layers |
|---|---|---|
| Lobby | The hotel reception | 0 (and 5, once you know) |
| The Shallow Halls (built) | The wading pools and swimming-lesson halls | 1 |
| The Deep End (gray-box) | The diving hall | 2 (the pipes, the yield clipboards, the first Halloran tape) |
| The Baths (future) | The steam rooms and hot soaks over the drain where it was found | 3 |
| The Survey Line (future) | F.A.L.C.O.N.'s sealed sectors, past the old resort | 4 |

## How it gets into the game

- **The sign-in book remembers the dead.** When a player dies in a round, their name appears in the
  lobby's register with no time out ("Personnel who do not sign out are considered still
  inside"). Cheap, personal, and a good first piece to build.
- **The unreachable vignettes**, at least one per level.
- **Hidden tapes:** Halloran's, collected in the levels and played in the lobby.
- **New lobby tapes:**
  - Tape 09, a cheerful internal promo selling deadwater to buyers;
  - Tape 00, damaged footage of the 1971 survey.
- **Props:** clipboards, memos, personnel files and resort brochures (the named-prop items
  already exist), plus Chlora-Cola's small print.
- **Layer 5 has consequences (idea):** once your team has found the reassignment list, the lobby
  changes a little each round. Your lockers are cleared, and a "Deep Survey" sign-up sheet has
  your names already filled in.
